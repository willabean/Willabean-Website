A complete working document covering the SEO review of willabean.co.uk and ready-to-paste copy rewrites for every page across the site, plus social media content for the first month.

**This document supersedes both earlier versions.** It reflects the correct material name (PVC-coated webbing, not Biothane — which is a registered trademark), the correct construction of every design (each one is built differently), the current product range (the Archie Pride has been discontinued), the full hardware range (silver, bronze, gold, rose gold, gunmetal), and which collars offer the buckle-or-clasp choice (every walking collar except the Willow and Bertie).

## 1. Quick technical fixes (do these first)

These are the highest-impact, lowest-effort changes. Tackle them this week before anything else.

**Fix the image alt text.** Every product and collection image currently has the same hashtag-style alt text on the existing site. Google treats this as near-empty and spammy, and it's useless for screen readers. Every image needs unique, descriptive alt text — for example, "Green 5L Mud Daddy portable dog washer with brush attachment" or "Brown cockapoo wearing olive and tan two-colour Walter collar". When Claude Code builds the new site, make sure descriptive alt text is part of every product image upload.

**Fix the title tag pattern.** Every page on the existing site ends with `– Willabean` in the title tag, which wastes valuable front-of-title space. Lead with the keyword, not the brand. Every title rewrite in this document does this properly.

**Add a price-advantage block to branded-product pages.** Your homepage banner says "Our branded products cost less than buying directly" — but the Mud Daddy product page doesn't mention it anywhere. Add a visible "Why buy from Willabean" block above the fold on every third-party product page (Mud Daddy, Sporting Saint, Polartec coats).

**Submit your sitemap to Google Search Console.** When the new site goes live, submit `/sitemap.xml` and request indexing on every page. Shopify and most modern site builders generate the sitemap automatically.

**Set up Product + Review schema.** Your existing site mentions 5.0 Google Reviews, but the stars probably aren't appearing in your search results. Make sure the new site's product pages have proper Product schema with AggregateRating and Review properties. Test with Google's Rich Results Test tool.

## 2. Overall SEO findings

**Brand confusion is a ranking headwind.** There are at least three other "Willa Bean" brands online (willabeanandco.com, willandbea.co.uk, @willabeanandco_ on Instagram). Every page needs to reinforce *what* Willabean sells and *where* (UK dog accessories), so Google clusters you correctly. Make sure your Organization schema lists country=GB and a physical address.

**Use accurate material terminology and lean into the keywords customers actually search for.** "PVC-coated dog collar UK", "waterproof dog collar UK" and "handmade dog collar UK" are the high-intent search terms that fit your products honestly. Your direct competitors (Collared Creatures, Trinkety Paws, Cooper & Me) all target similar terms. The rewrites below thread these naturally through the copy.

**Product page titles should lead with construction or category keywords, not dog names.** "The Mabel Dog Collar" tells Google nothing. Better: "Mabel Two-Colour Halved Dog Collar | Handmade UK". Keep the proprietary name for brand, pair it with descriptive keywords.

**No informational blog content yet.** This is where competitors win long-tail traffic. The three outlines in Section 11 fix this.

**Copy should sell the offer, not the spec sheet.** "Abrasion-resistant webbing" is a feature. "Comes home muddy, leaves looking brand new" is a promise. Every rewrite applies this shift.

## 3. The range — how Willabean's products fit together

Your range has seven collar designs, each with its own construction approach, purpose and customer. This isn't a flat catalogue — it's a proper ladder. That's a competitive advantage worth communicating clearly.

**The house collar — Bertie.** Single colour PVC-coated webbing, O-ring fastening, for indoor wear only. Not a walking collar.

**The everyday walking collar — Hugo.** Single colour PVC-coated webbing, full-length one-piece construction. Buckle or clasp. The first proper walking collar — where most new Willabean customers start.

**The fully layered — Walter.** A narrow strip of webbing riveted along the full length of a wider base strip. Two colours, the whole length of the collar. The understated, tailored design. Buckle or clasp.

**The accent design — Winnie.** A primary colour for the length of the collar, with a small layer of a second colour added at the buckle/clasp end as a pop of contrast. Buckle or clasp.

**The halved design — Mabel.** Two colours laid end-to-end and joined at the centre. One half is one colour, the other half is the other. Buckle or clasp.

**The sectional slip — Willow.** Three sections of PVC-coated webbing (three colours, or three sections of a single colour if preferred), separated by a D-ring and an O-ring that form the working slip mechanism. The colours are structural, not decorative — each one sits between hardware. The only collar in the range without buckle or clasp options, because the slip mechanism is the closure.

**The bespoke flagship — Archie.** Up to six colours of webbing layered onto a base colour and riveted in place. Seven colours in total if the customer wants them all. The most customisable collar in the range. Buckle or clasp.

**Hardware finishes (across the range):** silver, bronze, gold, rose gold, gunmetal.

**Every collar (aside from the Bertie) is custom-made to the customer's exact neck measurement** — not a size range. This is central to the brand promise and appears on every product page.

**Matching leads:** every walking collar (Hugo, Walter, Winnie, Mabel, Archie) has a matching lead. The Willow's matching lead is in development. The Bertie is a house collar and doesn't have one. The Henry Traffic Handle is a standalone close-control secondary handle, not tied to any specific design.

### Practical tips for posting

**Schedule, don't post live.** Use the Meta Business Suite (free) to schedule a week's worth of posts in one sitting.

**Best posting times for UK pet audience:** Tuesday-Thursday between 7-9pm. Sunday morning around 10am also performs well.

**Reels first.** If you can only do one type of content, do Reels. Instagram is currently rewarding video reach over still photos.

**Don't read the analytics for the first month.** Read them at 6 weeks. Looking before then will just make you stop.

**Reply to every comment.** Even a heart emoji counts. Spend ten minutes a day replying.

## 13. Rollout and internal linking strategy

**Publish cadence for the blog posts.** Post 1 (measurement guide) first. Three weeks later, Post 2 (PVC-coated webbing). Three weeks later, Post 3 (slip vs buckle). Total rollout about ten weeks.

**Internal linking is how the blog posts earn their keep.** Every product page should link out to the measurement guide. Every layered collar product page should link to the coated-webbing explainer. The Willow page should prominently link to the slip vs buckle post. Keep links contextual and inside body text rather than in a "related reading" box at the bottom — Google values inline links far more.

**Add FAQ schema** to every blog post. Most modern site builders support it.

**Request indexing in Search Console** after each post goes live.

---

## 14. Technical SEO checklist

When the new website Claude Code is building goes live, check the following.

- **Sitemap submitted** in Search Console and all product/collection URLs indexed.
- **Product schema + AggregateRating + Review** firing on every product page. Test with Google Rich Results Test.
- **Core Web Vitals on mobile** — LCP under 2.5s, CLS under 0.1.
- **Robots.txt** doesn't accidentally block `/collections/` or `/products/`.
- **HTTPS clean** — no mixed content warnings.
- **Canonical tags** point to the correct URL, not duplicate variant URLs.
- **hreflang or clear UK-only signal.**
- **Image alt text** is unique and descriptive on every image.
- **Title tags** lead with keywords, not the brand name.
- **Consistent NAP** (name, address, phone) across Google Business Profile, Trustpilot, UK pet directories.

## 15. Style principles for future rewrites

When you rewrite other products yourself (Christmas collection, gift vouchers, pet portraits), these habits will do most of the heavy lifting.

**Open with a scene or a moment the customer recognises, not with the product name.**

**Replace every "features" heading with a more emotive one.** "What makes the Mabel, the Mabel", "Made for", "Why dog owners choose this one".

**For every technical feature, write the customer-facing version alongside it.** "Zero stretch webbing" is a spec; "holds its exact fit no matter how hard your dog tests it" is a promise.

**Use specific nouns, not generic ones.** Not "outdoor adventures" but "river swims, woodland trails, rolling in something unspeakable".

**Close soft, not loud.** Point to the measurements guide, answer the next likely question, or mention lead time.

**For third-party branded products, always lead with "why buy it from us".** Price, dispatch speed, UK-based support.

**Every page needs to weave in the UK + handmade + made-to-measure story.** Brand differentiation from the other "Willa Bean" brands, and differentiation from the UK competition.

**Keep naming the construction.** Each design has its own build — Hugo single colour, Walter layered, Winnie accent, Mabel halved, Willow sectional slip, Archie multi-layered. Don't collapse them into one story. The fact that each is built differently *is* the story.

**Use accurate material terminology.** "PVC-coated webbing" or "waterproof coated webbing". Not Biothane (a registered trademark you don't buy from) or any close variant. The accurate terms work just as well for SEO.
