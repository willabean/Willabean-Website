How to measure your dog for a collar that actually fits

A collar that doesn't fit is worse than no collar at all, it chafes, it slips, or it chokes. The good news is measuring properly takes two minutes. Here's how.

Why the fit matters more than people realise

A collar that's too tight restricts breathing and rubs fur bald over time. A collar that's too loose slides up over the jawline or down over the shoulders, at best your dog's ID tag goes missing; at worst, they're out of the collar and loose in a car park. And a collar that sits too low on the neck pulls on the trachea every time your dog leans into the lead. None of this is dramatic in the way a bad lead attachment is dramatic, but all of it adds up to an unhappy dog.

What you need

  • A soft tape measure (a dressmaker's tape, not a rigid ruler)
  • A willing dog, standing still
  • A pocket of treats to keep them willing

That's it. If you don't have a soft tape, a length of string and a ruler work just as well.

The two-finger rule

Measure where the collar will actually sit: high on the neck, just behind the ears and above the shoulders. Not halfway down the neck, and definitely not over the windpipe. Wrap the tape around, keeping it snug but not tight, then slide two fingers flat between the tape and your dog's neck. That's the target fit.

Record the number where the tape overlaps itself. That's the number we need. If you're ordering from us, just type that number into the neck measurement field on the product page, we'll cut the collar to that exact size.

Common mistakes (and how to avoid them)

Measuring over thick fur

Long-haired breeds puff out at the neck, and a tape measure sitting on top of the fur gives you a neck that's 2–3cm bigger than it actually is. Press the tape down gently into the fur until you feel the dog's neck underneath.

Measuring while the dog is pulling away

A dog who's trying to turn their head to sniff the tape is also tensing the muscles you're trying to measure. Get them sitting, calm, with a treat, it's worth the extra thirty seconds.

Using a rigid ruler

A wooden or plastic ruler can't wrap around a neck. The measurement you get off a held-flat straight ruler is approximate at best. Soft tape or string-and-ruler only.

Not re-measuring after a haircut

Short-back-and-sides can take 2cm off a dog's apparent neck size overnight. Re-measure after any significant groom.

Puppy considerations

Puppies grow fast. Measure every 6–8 weeks up to about a year, and expect to buy at least one new collar during that time. Breed-specific quirks

Sighthounds (whippets, greyhounds, lurchers)

Sighthounds have heads narrower than their necks, a standard buckle collar can slip off over the head when they spook. Use a wider, martingale-style collar, or a well-fitted house collar with an ID tag and walk on a harness. Don't use a slip collar on a sighthound untrained, they panic and can injure themselves.

Bully breeds and broad-necks

A narrow 20mm collar looks wrong and can dig in on a Staffie or Bullie neck. Go wider (25mm+) for the proportion, and be particular about exact fit, broader necks need less slack, not more.

Spaniels and retrievers

Heavy ear furnishings sit where the collar does. Measure pressing the tape slightly below the ears, the collar will naturally push the feathering aside once it's on, and if in doubt, go 0.5cm tighter than your measurement to account for the hair you can't flatten.

What width should I choose?

Wider collars spread pressure over a bigger area, good for strong dogs, big breeds, and dogs who pull. Narrower collars look tidier on fine-necked breeds and small dogs. If you're unsure which width suits your dog, send us a photo with the breed and we'll suggest the right proportion.

What if your dog is between sizes?

With a custom-made collar this almost never happens, we cut to your measurement. But if you're ordering from a stocked range somewhere else and your dog is between 34cm and 36cm, go for the larger. A collar that's 1cm too big can be adjusted with the hardware; a collar that's 1cm too small can't be helped.

Frequently asked questions

How tight should a dog collar be?

You should be able to slide two fingers flat between the collar and your dog's neck. Any tighter and it restricts breathing; any looser and it can slip over the head, especially on sighthounds and deep-chested breeds.

Where should a dog collar sit on the neck?

High on the neck, just behind the ears and above the shoulders, not low where it sits over the windpipe. A collar that rides low pulls on the trachea when the dog leans into a lead.

How often should I re-measure my dog?

For puppies, every 6–8 weeks up to about a year. For adult dogs, re-measure after any significant weight change and after a short haircut, fur volume affects perceived neck size.

Is a collar or harness better?

For most walks, a well-fitted collar works fine. Harnesses are recommended for dogs who pull hard, dogs with tracheal issues, and most sighthounds and small breeds. Many of our customers use a harness for walks and a Willabean collar for ID tags.

Can I measure with string instead of a tape?

Yes, wrap a length of string around your dog's neck where the collar sits, mark the overlap, then lay it flat against a ruler. A soft tape is faster, but the string method is perfectly accurate.

What's the difference between neck circumference and collar size?

Neck circumference is the measurement around your dog's neck. Collar size refers to the usable length of the collar itself. With a custom collar like ours, we build the collar to your dog's measurement directly, you don't need to translate.
