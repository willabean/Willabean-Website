## 1. Quick technical fixes (do these first)

These are the highest-impact, lowest-effort changes on the site. Tackle them this week before anything else.

**Fix the image alt text.** Every product and collection image currently has the same hashtag-style alt text: `#willabean #dogleads #dogcollars #winnieclaspdogcollar #winniedoglead #hugodogcollar #hugodoglead`. Google treats this as near-empty and spammy, and it's useless for screen readers. Every image needs unique, descriptive alt text (for example, "Green 5L Mud Daddy portable dog washer with brush attachment" or "Brown cockapoo wearing green and tan two-colour layered Winnie collar"). Worth an hour of your time.

**Fix the title tag pattern.** Every page currently ends with `– Willabean` in the title tag, which wastes valuable front-of-title space. Lead with the keyword, not the brand. Every title rewrite in this document does this properly.

**Add the price-advantage message to branded-product pages.** Your homepage banner says "Our branded products cost less than buying directly" — but your Mud Daddy product page doesn't mention it anywhere. Add a visible "Why buy from Willabean" block above the fold on every third-party product page (Mud Daddy, Sporting Saint, Polartec coats).

**Submit your sitemap to Google Search Console.** Shopify generates it automatically at `/sitemap.xml`, but you need to submit it in Search Console for active tracking. While you're in there, request indexing on every page you rewrite.

**Check for Product + Review schema.** Your homepage mentions 5.0 Google Reviews. Those stars should be appearing in your search results (rich snippets). If they're not, your Product and AggregateRating schema isn't firing properly. Test with Google's Rich Results Test tool.

## 2. Overall SEO findings

**Brand confusion is a ranking headwind.** There are at least three other "Willa Bean" brands competing for your name (willabeanandco.com, willandbea.co.uk, @willabeanandco_ on Instagram). Every page needs to reinforce *what* Willabean sells and *where* (UK dog accessories), so Google clusters you correctly. Make sure your Organization schema lists country=GB and a physical address.

**The word "Biothane" belongs in your product copy.** "Biothane dog collar UK" is a high-intent search term your direct competitors (Collared Creatures, Trinkety Paws, Cooper & Me, Liv Like Busby) all target. Your current copy uses "PVC-coated webbing" and "waterproof webbing" but never "Biothane" itself — except in a customer review. If it's genuine Biothane-brand, use the name; if it's a generic equivalent, use "biothane-style". All the rewrites below do this.

**Product page titles should lead with construction keywords, not dog names.** "The Mabel Dog Collar" tells Google nothing about what it is. Better: "Mabel Two-Colour Layered Biothane Dog Collar | Handmade UK". Keep the proprietary name for brand, pair it with descriptive keywords.

**No informational blog content yet.** This is where your competitors win long-tail traffic. The three outlines in Section 11 fix this.

**Copy hard-sells features when it should be selling the offer.** "Abrasion-resistant webbing" is a spec. "Comes home muddy, leaves looking brand new" is a promise. Every rewrite applies this shift.

## 3. The range — how Willabean's products fit together

Your range is actually five distinct construction tiers, and this is a genuine competitive advantage that the current site doesn't communicate. Most UK handmade dog collar makers sell a flat range of similar products at similar prices. You have a proper ladder — which lets a customer enter cheaply, trust the brand, and come back for something bigger next time.

**The house collar — Bertie.** Single colour biothane, O-ring fastening, for indoor wear only. Not a walking collar.

**The everyday walking collar — Hugo.** Single-layer biothane, buckle or clasp. The first proper walking collar — where most new Willabean customers start.

**The understated layered — Walter.** A thinner strip of biothane riveted over a wider base strip. Two colours, but quietly — the tailored option in the layered range.

**The two-colour layered — Winnie, Mabel.** Two colours of biothane, layered and riveted in equal widths. The Winnie is the signature two-tone; the Mabel is the bold half-and-half design, available in two widths.

**The three-colour layered — Willow.** Three colours, layered and riveted, built as a slip collar with grab section and safety stopper. Purpose-built for gundog work.

**The bespoke flagship — Archie.** Up to six additional colours layered onto a base colour of your choice. Seven colours in total, if you want them all. The most customisable collar in the range.

**The fixed statement — Archie Pride.** Same construction as the Archie, but locked to the six Pride flag colours. Not a customisation — a specific celebration piece.

**All collars (aside from Bertie) come with matching leads.** The Willow is the one exception — a matching Willow lead is in development. The Henry Traffic Handle is a standalone close-control secondary handle, not tied to any specific design.

**Every collar is available in buckle or clasp fastening** (aside from the Bertie O-ring), with four hardware finishes: silver, bronze, gold or black.

**Every collar is made to the customer's exact neck measurement**, not a size range. This is central to the brand promise and should appear on every product page.

## 12. Rollout and internal linking strategy

**Publish cadence.** Post 1 (measurement guide) first. Three weeks later, Post 2 (biothane). Three weeks later, Post 3 (slip vs buckle). Total rollout about ten weeks. Google needs time to crawl, index and rank each one.

**Internal linking is how these earn their keep.** Every product page should link out to the measurement guide. Every collar product page should link to the biothane explainer. The Willow page should prominently link to the slip vs buckle post. The blog posts themselves should link to each other contextually.

**Avoid one linking pattern.** Don't stick every link at the bottom of the post in a "related reading" block. Google values contextual links inside the body text far more.

**Add FAQ schema.** The questions in each outline are written in the format Google likes.

**Request indexing in Search Console** after each post goes live.

## 14. Style principles for future rewrites

When you rewrite other products yourself (Christmas collection, gift vouchers, pet portraits), these habits will do most of the heavy lifting.

**Open with a scene or a moment the customer recognises, not with the product name.**

**Replace every "features" heading with a more emotive one.** "What makes the Mabel, the Mabel", "Made for", "Why dog owners choose this one".

**For every technical feature, write the customer-facing version alongside it.** "Zero stretch webbing" is a spec; "holds its exact fit no matter how hard your dog tests it" is a promise.

**Use specific nouns, not generic ones.** Not "outdoor adventures" but "river swims, woodland trails, rolling in something unspeakable".

**Close soft, not loud.** Point to the measurements guide, answer the next likely question, or mention lead time.

**For third-party branded products, always lead with "why buy it from us".** Price, dispatch speed, UK-based support.

**Every page needs to weave in the UK + handmade + layered-construction story.** Brand differentiation from the other "Willa Bean" brands, and differentiation from the UK biothane competition.

**Use the word "commissioned" sparingly but deliberately.** Your customers aren't buying collars — they're commissioning them. That vocabulary choice positions you as a craftsperson, not a retailer.

**Keep naming the construction.** "Layered and riveted" is your signature build. Every layered product page should mention it. The repetition becomes brand rather than boilerplate.
